If a bookmaker offers you 2.50 odds on a team winning, they're essentially saying "for every $1 you bet, you get $2.50 back if you win."
Flipping that around — if the payout is 2.50x your money, the bookmaker is implying the team has a 1 in 2.50 chance of winning, which is 40%.

The Kelly Criterion answers one question: given that I have an edge, how much of my bankroll should I bet?
Too little and you're leaving money on the table. Too much and one bad run wipes you out. Kelly gives you the mathematically optimal stake.

def kelly_fraction(model_prob, odds_decimal, fraction=0.25):
    edge = model_prob - (1 / odds_decimal)
    kelly = edge / (odds_decimal - 1)
    return max(0, kelly * fraction)

edge = model_prob - (1 / odds_decimal)
Your edge is how much more confident you are than the bookmaker. If your model says 60% and the bookmaker implies 50%, your edge is 10%.

kelly = edge / (odds_decimal - 1)
Divides the edge by the net odds — how much you win per $1 staked. At 1.91 odds you win $0.91 per $1. This gives you the raw Kelly fraction — the percentage of your bankroll to bet.

return max(0, kelly * fraction)
Two things here. max(0, ...) means if the edge is negative, bet nothing — never bet against yourself. The * fraction multiplies by 0.25, meaning you only bet a quarter of what full Kelly suggests. This is called fractional Kelly and exists because:

Full Kelly assumes your model probabilities are perfectly accurate — they never are
Full Kelly leads to massive swings in bankroll even when you have a real edge
Most professional bettors use half Kelly or quarter Kelly to stay conservative
---
When we first ran Kelly the bankroll grew to trillions of dollars. That happened because:

Kelly compounds — every bet stakes a percentage of the current bankroll, so winnings get reinvested immediately
Over 900 bets even a small consistent edge grows exponentially when fully reinvested
Our model probabilities were overconfident, so Kelly was suggesting stakes that were too large

This is why we switched to flat betting first. Flat betting gives you a honest picture of whether your edge is real before you start compounding it aggressively.

Drawdown measures the worst drop from a peak your bankroll experienced at any point during the season. Not the drop from the start — the drop from whatever the highest point was at that moment.
peak = history[0]
Start by assuming the first value is the highest point seen so far.
for value in history:
    if value > peak:
        peak = value
Loop through every bankroll value. If the current value is higher than the previous peak, update the peak. This tracks the running high watermark — the best your bankroll has ever been up to this point.
dd = (peak - value) / peak * 100
Calculate how far the current value has dropped from that peak, as a percentage. For example:
peak  = $2000
value = $1800
dd    = (2000 - 1800) / 2000 * 100 = 10%
if dd > max_dd:
    max_dd = dd
If this drawdown is the worst one seen so far, record it.
